Kicked-Up Portuguese Bifana

Portugal's everyday pork sandwich, turned up with paprika oil, grilled peppers, melted sheep's cheese, and crispy prosciutto.

Introduction

The bifana is Portugal's everyday pork sandwich: thin slices of garlicky marinated pork tucked into a crusty roll, usually with a swipe of yellow mustard. This version from Gordon Ramsay's Ramsay Around the World turns it up with paprika oil, grilled peppers, melted sheep's cheese, and crispy prosciutto. Learn more about the bifana on Wikipedia .

Makes 1 large sandwich, enough for 2 people. Plan on about 30 minutes of marinating time (longer is better) plus about 25 minutes of cooking. You will need a grill or grill pan and a cast iron skillet, both running hot at the same time.

Ingredients

For the pork marinade:

  • 1 large boneless pork chop, sliced thin into 3 pieces
  • 3 tablespoons mostarda (Italian mustard fruit preserve; whole-grain mustard works in a pinch)
  • 2 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • ¼ cup olive oil

For the sandwich:

  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • About ¼ cup olive oil, divided
  • 1 large loaf crusty bread, ends trimmed and sliced in half lengthwise
  • Kosher salt, to taste
  • 1 large red bell pepper, thickly sliced
  • 1 red onion, thickly sliced
  • 2 fresh chiles (ideally 1 red and 1 green), sliced
  • ½ cup chopped cilantro
  • ½ lemon
  • 2 slices prosciutto
  • 1 small ball semi-hard sheep's cheese, sliced ½ inch thick
  • Yellow mustard, for spreading
  • 1 handful arugula

Instructions

  1. Place the pork in a bowl and toss with the mostarda, garlic, and ¼ cup olive oil. Cover and marinate in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes , longer if you can.
  2. Heat a grill or grill pan over high heat . Set a cast iron skillet on the heat at the same time so both are hot when you need them.
  3. In a small bowl, mix the smoked paprika with 2 tablespoons of olive oil until smooth. Set this paprika oil aside; you will use it on the bread and the pork.
  4. Brush the cut sides of the bread generously with paprika oil and season with salt. Lay the bread cut-side down on the hot grill and toast until deeply browned and crispy. This crunchy base keeps the sandwich from going soggy.
  5. Put the bell pepper, red onion, and chiles in a bowl and season with salt. Add 1 tablespoon olive oil to the hot skillet, then add the vegetables. Toss, add a little more oil, and keep tossing as they brown. Once they are softened and caramelized, add the cilantro and a squeeze of lemon juice, toss, and transfer to a plate.
  6. Lay the marinated pork directly on the hot grill grates and season with salt. Brush the pieces all over with paprika oil. Flip after about 90 seconds , then season again with salt and brush with more paprika oil.
  7. Rub the prosciutto slices with olive oil and lay them on the grill until crisp, about 1 minute per side. Watch them closely ; they burn fast.
  8. Once the pork is cooked through, stack it with the cheese right on the grill: largest pork slice on the bottom, then a slice of cheese, then pork, then cheese, then the last pork slice. Cover the stack with an upside-down pan so the cheese melts.
  9. Spread a thin layer of yellow mustard on the insides of both bread halves. Spoon a generous amount of the pepper mixture onto the bottom half and lightly season with salt.
  10. When the cheese has melted, lift the pork stack off the grill and set it on top of the peppers. Top with a handful of arugula, then the crispy prosciutto. Add the top half of the bread, press down gently , slice the sandwich in half, and serve right away while the cheese is still melty.

Notes and variations

  • No mostarda? Whole-grain mustard works in a pinch.
  • Thirty minutes of marinating is the minimum. A few hours gives the pork more garlic and mustard flavor.
  • Keep the grill and the skillet hot at the same time so the vegetables, pork, and prosciutto finish together.
  • The prosciutto crisps in about a minute per side and burns fast, so stay close.
  • Serve right away. The toasted bread and melted cheese are at their best in the first few minutes.

Where to buy one

Don't want to fire up the grill? In Washington, Lonely Siren at Pike Place Market in Seattle serves a traditional bifana on house-made bread. On the East Coast, Mr. Bifana at Time Out Market New York slow-cooks Iberian pork and dips every roll in jus.
